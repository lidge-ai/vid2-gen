# Film grammar for a video agent that cannot watch or listen

**Purpose.** Use this as a rulebook for authoring and checking a JSON timeline, not as a claim that editing can be reduced to a score. A timeline can prove frame positions, durations, text geometry, cue placement and audio levels. It cannot by itself prove that a joke lands, a performance feels true, or a generated image matches a brief. Record those as editorial decisions and ask for a human review when the decision matters. Walter Murch explicitly treats readiness for a new idea as the reason to cut, and treats the visible blink only as a possible clue to that readiness. [Murch interview](https://www.filmsound.org/murch/interview-with-walter-murch.htm), [Murch on the blink](https://www.provideocoalition.com/aotc-murch-bcpc/amp/).

**Evidence labels used below:** **documented** = a named source says it; **measured** = a result for the stated sample; **proposed check** = a vid2-gen starting rule derived from the sources, not a universal film or platform standard. Check frame counts after `vid2 resolve timeline.json --json`, because scene overlap, rational frame rates and rounding can move boundaries; vid2's documented authoring path is `validate` → `resolve` → `preview` → `render` → `qa`. [vid2 README](https://github.com/lidge-ai/vid2-gen/blob/main/README.md), [timeline timing](https://github.com/lidge-ai/vid2-gen/blob/main/skills/vid2-timeline/references/time.md).

## 1. Decide why each cut exists: Murch, blink theory, and continuity

Murch's **Rule of Six** orders a cut's aims as emotion (51%), story (23%), rhythm (10%), eye trace (7%), two-dimensional screen plane (5%), and three-dimensional action space (4%). The percentages are an illustrative priority hierarchy, **not** measured quality weights to optimize. A collaborator who worked with Murch reports the full ordering; Murch's own account says a cut works when the viewer is ready for a new idea and that a scene change can merit a longer hold. [Collaborator's account](https://www.calibratefilms.com/post/reflections-from-the-edit-suite-lessons-learned-working-with-walter-murch), [Murch interview](https://www.filmsound.org/murch/interview-with-walter-murch.htm).

**Checkable translation (proposed):** every cut in the edit table must name (a) the idea or feeling established by the outgoing shot, (b) the new information in the incoming shot, and (c) the intended gaze point on both sides. If (a) and (b) are identical, try a continuous state change or lengthen the hold. Flag, but do not automatically reject, a cut whose gaze target jumps across the frame without a reveal or change of scale. Murch says he often holds the last shot of an outgoing scene about **30% longer** than he would for an intra-scene cut, and gives the incoming scene extra orientation time; use this as an optional chapter-boundary variant, not a blanket multiplier. [Murch interview](https://www.filmsound.org/murch/interview-with-walter-murch.htm).

**Blink theory, precisely:** Murch associates an actor's blink with a completed thought. In his 2019 explanation he says he generally cuts **one or two frames before** the blink, not on a frame in which the actor's eyes are closing; he also says to override the cue when the scene's rhythm calls for it. For animation or screen capture, substitute a semantic completion marker—typed command accepted, button response settled, result visible—for a biological blink. Do not use automated blink detection as a hard gate. [Murch interview](https://www.provideocoalition.com/aotc-murch-bcpc/amp/).

**Cut grammar (documented, then proposed check):**

| Edit | What it communicates | Timeline check for an agent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cut on action | An action continues through a change of viewpoint; the motion draws attention away from the seam. [Filmmaking guide](https://learnaboutfilm.com/film-language/editing/) | Tag the action's start, seam and finish. Require motion on both sides of the cut in the same direction; inspect seam stills at `cutFrame-1` and `cutFrame`. For a captured click, cut after the input is legible and before the response has entirely settled. This is a proposed rule, not a fixed frame offset. |
| Match cut | Two shots connect through similar framing, composition or action; color, camera movement and subject can also carry the match. Netflix researchers describe frame and motion matching as distinct types. [Netflix match-cut paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05766) | Store `matchAxis` such as `centroid`, `scale`, `direction`, `shape` or `color`, plus outgoing/incoming reference frames. Reject a claim of a match cut when no axis and pair of frames are specified. An agent can compare boxes or vectors, then ask a viewer to judge meaning. |
| J cut | Incoming scene audio begins **before** its image. [Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/trim-clips/perform-j-cuts-and-l-cuts.html) | Assert `incomingAudioStartFrame < pictureCutFrame`; preserve the prior scene's visible image until the cut. Use to announce a place, voice or action. |
| L cut | Outgoing scene audio continues **after** the next image appears. [Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/trim-clips/perform-j-cuts-and-l-cuts.html) | Assert `outgoingAudioEndFrame > pictureCutFrame`; make the tail end deliberate. Use to carry an explanation or ambience across the cut. |

There is **no sourced universal J/L overlap length**. Begin with a few frames to a fraction of a second, inspect the transcript and waveform, and keep a split edit only if it improves anticipation or continuity. Audio must remain on the absolute timeline across scene joins; vid2 already documents this for its mix. [Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/trim-clips/perform-j-cuts-and-l-cuts.html), [vid2 audio](https://github.com/lidge-ai/vid2-gen/blob/main/structure/audio.md).

## 2. Shot length: measure before choosing a pace

Barry Salt defines **average shot length (ASL)** as included running time in seconds divided by the number of shots, with explicit rules for credits, intertitles and projection speed. Cinemetrics recommends considering the **median and distribution** too: a few long holds can make a rapid montage's mean misleading. A scene made of text states, camera moves or dissolves is not automatically a series of separate film shots; decide what counts as a visual discontinuity before calculating ASL. [Salt's method](https://cinemetrics.uchicago.edu/article/9890e39c-ead0-49ff-a800-37d83723836a), [Cinemetrics on mean and median](https://cinemetrics.uchicago.edu/article/616e7ecc-7915-4768-b84d-7dec79aa77c2).

| Form and sample | Observed ASL, with limits | Use as a planning comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Film trailers, 15 trailers in a Japanese design study | **1.8 s** combined mean; its generated-trailer algorithm used **1.5–2 s** as a shot-length standard. This is an older, small trailer sample, not a present-day mandate. [Study, pp. 4–5](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jssdj/48/6/48_KJ00001647591/_pdf/-char/ja) | A plausible rapid trailer reference when shots are visually simple. |
| US horror trailers, 50 top-grossing titles from 2011–2015 | **0.97 s pooled mean**, **0.75 s pooled median**, **1.09 s mean of trailer means**. Per-trailer means span **0.55–2.72 s**; the 10th–90th percentile of those 50 means is **0.74–1.78 s**. These numbers are my reproducible arithmetic on Redfern's `US_Horror_Trailers_SL_Data.csv` (6,762 positive shot durations), not numbers quoted by the data author. Genre and shot-detection conventions matter. [Dataset and method](https://zenodo.org/records/4479068), [data paper](https://researchdatajournal.org/article/view/24675) | A fast horror-trailer reference; never generalize its lower end to legible UI proof shots. |
| Television commercials, historical US comparison | A scholarly review reports **3.8 s** in 1978 (7.9 cuts per 30-second spot) and **2.3 s** in 1991 (13.2 cuts). Those are period-specific TV measurements, not current social-ad norms. [Review citing MacLachlan and Logan, p. 59](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED473792.pdf) | Treat **roughly 2–4 s** as a historical comparison range; choose current pace from the actual ad and reading load. |
| Music videos, 10 Billboard top-single clips sampled in 2016 | Reported average is **1 s 18 frames at 25 fps = 1.72 s**, with **141 shots** in an average **4 min 1 s** clip. Individual video ASLs in the table range from **0 s 23 frames = 0.92 s** to **9 s 1 frame = 9.04 s**. The Mike Posner case has **118 shots** and about **1 s 24 frames = 1.96 s** ASL. [Pérez Rufí and Rodríguez-López, table 1 and case study](https://dspace.ceu.es/server/api/core/bitstreams/8af432e0-0c4d-38b8-e053-0100007fe1f5/content) | The wide range is the lesson: performance, narrative and music-driven videos pace differently. |

**Proposed check:** report `shot_count`, mean, median, p10/p90, and a per-section strip of durations. Warn on runs of very short shots carrying new text or a new product claim, then apply the reading-time test below. Do not reject a video for missing a genre's ASL. A long continuous kinetic move can have high ASL and still feel energetic; the reverse holds for static quick cuts. [Salt's definition](https://cinemetrics.uchicago.edu/article/9890e39c-ead0-49ff-a800-37d83723836a), [Cinemetrics distribution discussion](https://cinemetrics.uchicago.edu/article/616e7ecc-7915-4768-b84d-7dec79aa77c2).

## 3. Rhythm: beats, phrases, drops, and intentional off-beats

Music offers at least three grids: **song part**, **bar**, and **beat**. Final Cut Pro exposes each separately and treats song-part changes as especially useful edit points; Berklee recommends **2-, 4-, 8- or 16-bar phrases** as scoring units and notes that many sync points begin a new phrase rather than demanding a hit. A hard sync point such as a punch or cut may need a precise frame; a dissolve can tolerate a wider window. [Apple Final Cut Pro](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/guide/final-cut-pro/ver65b55a2b7/mac), [Berklee film scoring](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/5-film-scoring-concepts-to-know-for-your-first-movie-score/).

**Mechanical rules:** beat period = `60 / BPM` seconds; bar period = `meter × 60 / BPM`; frame = `round((offsetSeconds + beatIndex × 60/BPM) × fps)`. At **120 BPM, 4/4**, a beat is **0.5 s**, a bar **2 s**, 4 bars **8 s**, and 8 bars **16 s**; at 30 fps those are 15, 60, 240 and 480 frames. Resolve once on the output frame grid, then compare authored cut frames with the resolved downbeat. Vid2's `beat: {"bpm":120,"offset":"0s","meter":4}`, `vid2 audio beats song.mp3 --json`, and `vid2 audio snap 12.3 --bpm 120 --offset 0 --json` are existing authoring aids; the detected grid's downbeat should be reviewed before committing it. [vid2 audio skill](https://github.com/lidge-ai/vid2-gen/blob/main/skills/vid2-audio/SKILL.md), [vid2 timing](https://github.com/lidge-ai/vid2-gen/blob/main/skills/vid2-timeline/references/time.md).

**Proposed editorial policy:** mark a 4- or 8-bar phrase boundary for a new chapter, color field or claim. Put the decisive product action, match-cut seam or impact on a chosen downbeat or musical accent, ideally within **±1 output frame** when strict sync is intended. Let explanatory shots, voice and readable text cut **between** beats when the action or thought finishes there; write `syncIntent: "offbeat"` so the QA report distinguishes choice from drift. At 120 BPM a halfway-between-beats cut is 0.25 s after a beat (about 6/8/15 frames at 24/30/60 fps). Use consecutive beat-level cuts for a short acceleration, then restore a longer hold; cutting every beat for the whole film makes the edit mechanically predictable. The ±1-frame tolerance and short-burst policy are proposed vid2 checks, while the distinction among song parts, bars and beats is documented. [Apple Final Cut Pro](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/guide/final-cut-pro/ver65b55a2b7/mac), [Berklee film scoring](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/5-film-scoring-concepts-to-know-for-your-first-movie-score/).

**Drop recipe (proposed):** label `buildStart`, `preDrop`, `drop`, and `release`; end the riser at `drop`, put the picture reveal and impact on that exact frame, and optionally pull the music bed back for **half to one beat** before it. At 120 BPM this creates **0.25–0.5 s** of contrast, with voice and essential UI sounds protected. An off-beat cut just before a drop can create anticipation; the drop frame carries the answer. These durations are design trials, not published acoustic thresholds. Berklee's hard/soft sync distinction and Murch's account of deliberately using silence support the underlying choice. [Berklee](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/5-film-scoring-concepts-to-know-for-your-first-movie-score/), [Murch on silence](https://www.filmsound.org/murch/soundfilmman.htm).

## 4. The 12 animation principles, translated into UI and kinetic type

Johnston and Thomas formulated the 12 principles for character animation; Adobe explicitly notes their use beyond film, including web design. The applications and checks in the right column below are **proposed translations** for a stage renderer, not claims that Disney prescribed interface timings. [Adobe's full principles guide](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/animation/discover/principles-of-animation.html), [Disney animation process](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/animation/).

| Principle | UI / typography application and check |
| --- | --- |
| Squash and stretch | A chip or icon can compress briefly on a hit, then recover. Keep text glyph proportions intact; if a shape stretches, check that its area/visual mass remains plausible. |
| Anticipation | Before a full-screen expand or number reveal, create a small preparatory move or audio rise; verify the prep begins before the main event. |
| Staging | One visual claim at a time. Test that the decisive product/control and main title are visible in a still of the settled frame. |
| Straight ahead / pose to pose | Author key states for headline, product action and result, then interpolate; reserve procedural per-frame motion for particles, cursor trails or texture. Check all key poses first. |
| Follow through / overlapping action | Let a glow, shadow or trailing accent settle after its parent; stagger related words in reading order. Check that the secondary tail does not cover the next claim. |
| Slow in / slow out | Use entrance deceleration and exit acceleration rather than uniform speed for most UI changes; check chosen easing per property. |
| Arcs | Cursor and camera paths can arc toward a control; verify that the arc ends on the target, while a linear path remains available for precise UI elements. |
| Secondary action | A caret blink or subtle highlight may support typing; cap concurrent motion so the proof action stays dominant. |
| Timing | Store durations in frames and ensure the readable hold remains after entrances and before exits. |
| Exaggeration | Scale or accent a single proof moment enough to survive phone playback, then return to the normal system. Record the emphasized moment. |
| Solid drawing | Preserve text geometry, UI alignment, perspective and thickness from key pose to key pose; check for clipped glyphs and collapsing layout. |
| Appeal | Use a consistent silhouette, type system, palette and motion vocabulary across scenes; compare the contact sheet as a set. |

**Documented motion tokens and frame translations.** Rounding below is to the nearest whole output frame and is only a conversion (`frames = round(seconds × fps)`), not an instruction to use every token. Carbon uses productive and expressive motion; it warns against decorative bounce/stretch in product UI and supplies exact curves and duration tokens. Material 3 supplies distinct standard and emphasized curves plus duration tokens. Apple publishes a **SwiftUI default spring** with `response: 0.55`, `dampingFraction: 1.0`, `blendDuration: 0.0` on current OS versions; it is a spring response parameter, **not a universal 550 ms transition duration**. UIKit's `animate(withDuration:animations:)` uses `curveEaseInOut` when options are omitted. [Carbon motion](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/), [Material Android motion attributes](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Motion.md), [Apple SwiftUI default](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/animation/default.md), [Apple UIKit animation API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiview/animate(withduration:animations:).md).

| Reference token / use | Seconds | Frames @ 24 / 30 / 60 fps |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Carbon `duration-fast-01`, button/toggle | 0.070 | 2 / 2 / 4 |
| Carbon `duration-fast-02`, fade | 0.110 | 3 / 3 / 7 |
| Carbon `duration-moderate-01`, small expansion | 0.150 | 4 / 5 / 9 |
| Carbon `duration-moderate-02`, expansion/toast | 0.240 | 6 / 7 / 14 |
| Carbon `duration-slow-01`, large expansion | 0.400 | 10 / 12 / 24 |
| Carbon `duration-slow-02`, background dimming | 0.700 | 17 / 21 / 42 |
| Material `motionDurationShort1` to `Short4` | 0.050–0.200 | 1–5 / 2–6 / 3–12 |
| Material `motionDurationMedium1` to `Medium4` | 0.250–0.400 | 6–10 / 8–12 / 15–24 |
| Material `motionDurationLong1` to `Long4` | 0.450–0.600 | 11–14 / 14–18 / 27–36 |

Token names, values and use examples come from [Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/) and [Material 3's Android implementation](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Motion.md). For a concrete curve preset, Carbon productive entrance is `cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0.38, 0.9)`, productive exit is `(0.2, 0, 1, 0.9)`, and expressive standard is `(0.4, 0.14, 0.3, 1)`. Material 3 standard is `(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`, emphasized decelerate is `(0.05, 0.7, 0.1, 1)`, and its full emphasized path is a **two-segment path**, not one cubic Bézier: `M 0,0 C 0.05,0 0.133333,0.06 0.166666,0.4 C 0.208333,0.82 0.25,1 1,1`. Preserve the source curve when borrowing the name. [Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/), [Material 3](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Motion.md).

**Proposed renderer check:** resolve every animation to integer start/end frames; warn if a purported movement lasts fewer than two output frames; require an explicit `hold` after a kinetic title settles. Keep UI feedback quick and save expressive, screen-scale motion for an important change. A video may use more expressive motion than an actual product interface, but its movements still need a clear semantic owner. [Carbon's productive/expressive distinction](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/).

## 5. Kinetic typography: reading, scale, safe areas, contrast

Reading numbers should be **borrowed cautiously from timed-text research**. Netflix's English subtitle templates cap adult reading speed at **17 characters/second** and children's at **15 characters/second**; its general minimum subtitle event is **5/6 s** (20 frames at 24 fps) and maximum is **7 s**. A BBC Research study of 25 frequent subtitle users found block subtitles rated “good” near **177 words/minute = 2.95 words/second**, with scrolling text near 171 WPM. Neither result is a validated limit for animated advertising headlines, which compete with moving imagery. [Netflix reading-speed guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/219375728-Timed-Text-Style-Guide-Subtitle-Templates), [Netflix timing guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/215758617-Timed-Text-Style-Guide-General-Requirements), [BBC Research study](https://downloads.bbc.co.uk/rd/pubs/whp/whp-pdf-files/WHP306.pdf).

**Proposed check:** for each *new* text state, compute `neededSeconds = max(0.833, visibleCharacters/17, words/2.95)` for adult English, starting only when all required glyphs have become legible; use `15` characters/s for child-directed English. Compare that with the **fully legible hold**, excluding entrance/exit frames. Example: a 34-character, six-word claim needs `max(0.833, 2.0, 2.03) = 2.03 s`, or about 49/61/122 frames at 24/30/60 fps. If the title must be understood while the product action also changes, add a hold or split the claim. This formula is a conservative adaptation of subtitle figures, not a Netflix rule for launch films. [Netflix limits](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/219375728-Timed-Text-Style-Guide-Subtitle-Templates), [BBC study](https://downloads.bbc.co.uk/rd/pubs/whp/whp-pdf-files/WHP306.pdf).

For an **opening hook**, prefer a short phrase with one dominant word and reveal the rest after the viewer knows where to look. Do not make each word a disposable one-frame flash: a word-by-word build still needs a stable final state. Vid2's current direction reference proposes 72–96 px headlines, 38–52 px support text and 28–36 px labels at 1920×1080, with a 1.2 s minimum large-title hold; treat these as *existing vid2 house defaults*, not externally validated universal type sizes. Use a 5% margin as a baseline on plain 16:9 delivery (96 px left/right, 54 px top/bottom at 1920×1080), then apply the destination platform's actual overlay mask. [vid2 typography reference](https://github.com/lidge-ai/vid2-gen/blob/main/skills/vid2-direction/references/typography.md).

YouTube provides downloadable **horizontal, vertical and square safe-zone templates** because UI overlays vary by placement; TikTok says its safe zone changes with orientation, caption length and ad format. Therefore **5% alone does not certify vertical social placement**. Keep logo, CTA and essential product UI inside the appropriate template mask and test the actual surface. YouTube lists 1920×1080, 1080×1920 and 1080×1080 as recommended HD dimensions for horizontal, vertical and square ads. [Google Ads specs and masks](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13547298?hl=en-AU), [TikTok in-feed safe zones](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/tiktok-auction-in-feed-ads?lang=en-GB).

For text over pixels, use the WCAG reference threshold of **4.5:1** for normal text and **3:1** for large text; W3C defines large text as at least **18 pt regular or 14 pt bold** (approximately 24 or 18.5 CSS px). Those are web accessibility thresholds, useful as a video QA floor, not a promise that a moving, compressed, phone-sized title is readable. Evaluate the **worst background during the whole text span**, not only a settled still; add an opaque plate or local darkening if needed. Vid2 currently checks settled stage text against 4.5:1 below 40 authored px and 3:1 above, while its ordinary text QA uses a 3:1 check: the research supports making the policy explicit and consistent by text role and output size. [W3C WCAG explanation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/contrast-minimum), [vid2 QA](https://github.com/lidge-ai/vid2-gen/blob/main/structure/qa.md), [vid2 stage QA](https://github.com/lidge-ai/vid2-gen/blob/main/structure/stage.md).

## 6. Color script and grading for a launch film

Pixar describes a **colorscript** as a sequence of small scene paintings that establishes a film's color language. For *Monsters, Inc.*, its artists contrasted a drab industrial town with bright monsters. Pixar's RenderMan tutorial distinguishes **correction** (match white balance, luminance and saturation across shots) from **grading** (choose an emotional look). Google's YouTube production guide likewise asks for color contrast between subject and set and for brand colors to inform props and identity. [Pixar colorscript](https://www.pixar.com/monsters-inc), [Pixar RenderMan grading](https://renderman.pixar.com/the-grand-tour-compositing), [Google production guide](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/17033/youtube_ad_production_guide_external.pdf).

**Proposed color-script record:** one thumbnail per scene with `sceneId`, narrative function, dominant/background/accent colors, neutral reference (skin, product UI white or gray), contrast strategy, grade ID, and transition into the next scene. Sketch a **problem → solution → proof → CTA** color progression, for example restrained/dim → accent appears → clean product colors → brand lockup. That progression is an editorial proposal, not a required color psychology mapping. Check that scene thumbnails form a coherent sequence, that adjacent shots do not jump in exposure or white balance accidentally, and that UI screenshots preserve brand and status colors unless an intentional grade is documented. [Pixar colorscript](https://www.pixar.com/monsters-inc), [Pixar correction versus grade](https://renderman.pixar.com/the-grand-tour-compositing).

**Machine checks:** sample representative and seam frames; record mean luma, saturation, black/white clipping and text contrast per scene; compare consecutive frames and flag outliers for review. If a palette jump coincides with a chapter, mark it intentional. Apply a shared base grade to generated stills before shot-specific creative looks, and keep the proof capture neutral enough to verify real UI. Do not hard-code a Kelvin, saturation or color-distance pass/fail value without an output profile and a reviewed reference set; the cited sources provide a workflow, not those thresholds. Vid2 already has `grade`, `grain`, contact sheets and seam stills, so the immediate gap is a color-script comparison report. [vid2 schema reference](https://github.com/lidge-ai/vid2-gen/blob/main/skills/vid2-timeline/references/schema.md), [vid2 QA](https://github.com/lidge-ai/vid2-gen/blob/main/structure/qa.md).

## 7. Trailer and product-launch structure

The **1–3 second hook** is a useful *social-ad target*, not a film-wide law. TikTok's ad guide says to grab attention in the **first three seconds**, while its education playbook gives a **3–5 s hook**, **10–20 s key-message body** and **last 3–5 s CTA or earlier**. Google's YouTube production guide instead says to hook viewers in the **first five seconds** and suggests **at least two shots in those five seconds** for its ad format. These recommendations concern particular distribution settings and can coexist. [TikTok ad guide](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-AU/guides/video-advertising-guide), [TikTok education playbook](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/TikTokSMB_Education_Playbook2024.pdf), [Google YouTube production guide](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/17033/youtube_ad_production_guide_external.pdf).

**Proposed 30-second product-launch beat sheet** (example, not a sourced universal template):

| Time | Required evidence | Example for an agent-authored CLI launch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 s | Hook shows a specific tension, result or surprising action. | `"A video edit is a file"` appears over a result, or a command begins a visibly real render. |
| 3–8 s | Problem: one obstacle the viewer recognizes. | Show the time or complexity of manual editing with a single readable claim. |
| 8–18 s | Solution in use: one or two decisive actions, not a feature list. | A JSON timeline becomes a real captured UI sequence; align an action to a phrase turn. |
| 18–25 s | Proof: visible input → output, comparison, or verifiable result. | Real app capture, a before/after, render report or finished frame. Avoid a decorative generated scene as sole evidence. |
| 25–30 s | Brand, CTA and readable hold. | Name, command or URL and a specific next action; hold long enough for the text test. |

This is a practical adaptation of TikTok's hook–body–close / problem–solution advice and Google's emphasis on product-in-use, multiple peaks and a specific CTA. Google warns against relying solely on the old lead-in/build/climax/reveal TV arc for YouTube; it recommends a strong opening cue, later peaks and brand cues throughout. [TikTok creative guidance](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/tiktok_creative_accelerator/pc/en), [Google ABCD guide](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/8472/ABCD_Complete_V7b_HR_1.pdf), [Google production guide](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/17033/youtube_ad_production_guide_external.pdf).

**Documented examples, with their limits:** TikTok's GRIVETY case reports that its fashion ads used hook → problem → solution → CTA; it is a structural example, not evidence that those exact seconds or a particular edit caused sales. [TikTok case study](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/inspiration/grivety-tiktok-smb-success-stories). Google's Ford case describes five-second openings built from real car-buyer pain points for YouTube. [Ford case study](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/intl/en-emea/marketing-strategies/video/fords-youtube-campaign-drives-results/). For a cinematic match-cut example, the Netflix match-cut paper discusses *2001: A Space Odyssey*'s bone-to-spaceship cut and a *Star Wars* trailer's matched jump action; use the **relationship** between images, not the borrowed imagery. [Netflix match-cut paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05766).

**Proposed checks:** first nonblank product-relevant image and meaningful text/action by 3 s for a social cutdown; a named proof scene before the final CTA; CTA text survives the reading-time test; and a muted contact-sheet pass still explains problem → action → result. If the only “proof” is generated illustration, report that the film has a claim but no demonstrated product action. [TikTok hook guidance](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-AU/guides/video-advertising-guide), [Google product-in-use guidance](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/17033/youtube_ad_production_guide_external.pdf).

## 8. Sound design: cues, space, ducking, and loudness

Adobe identifies whooshes, impacts and rumbles as common trailer transition effects, distinguishes hard-synced sounds from looser ambience, and recommends a cue sheet and deliberate fades. A game-trailer editor describes layering native action audio with transition SFX; Murch describes using deliberate silence to expose a character's internal state. These support a **purpose-driven** cue track rather than an impact on every cut. [Adobe SFX guide](https://www.adobe.com/uk/creativecloud/video/discover/sfx-for-video.html), [Frame.io game-trailer craft](https://blog.frame.io/2023/03/27/editing-game-promo-trailer/), [Murch on silence](https://www.filmsound.org/murch/soundfilmman.htm).

| Cue | Rule an agent can check | Intended effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Riser | Its **end** reaches the reveal/drop frame; store `anchor:"end"`. | Anticipation into a changed state. |
| Impact / click | Its **onset** is the action or picture-cut frame; store `anchor:"start"`. | Confirms an actual hit, click, result or drop. |
| Whoosh | Its **peak** passes through the motion or transition seam; store `anchor:"peak"`. | Carries movement, not a static title. |
| Sub drop | Pair low-frequency weight with an audible mid/high transient so a phone can communicate the hit; check that it does not mask the next spoken word. | Scale at a major reveal only. |
| Silence / pullback | Mark an intentional range immediately before a peak; protect essential voice and action audio. | Contrast and attention reset. |

The first three anchor names and semantics are **already vid2 behavior**, not industry-specified envelope durations; `audio.cues[]` accepts `{"at":"12s","sfx":"preset:impact","anchor":"start","volume":0.7}` and auto cues already cover several transitions and stage events. Do not double an automatic impact with an authored one without inspecting the cue plan. [vid2 audio contract](https://github.com/lidge-ai/vid2-gen/blob/main/structure/audio.md), [vid2 audio direction](https://github.com/lidge-ai/vid2-gen/blob/main/skills/vid2-audio/SKILL.md).

**Mix rule:** keep dialogue, music and SFX as separate stems; duck music under voice with a sidechain or authored gain envelope, then check whether words are intelligible. FFmpeg's `sidechaincompress` takes the music as its first input and the voice/key signal as its second input; it does not prescribe one universally correct attack, release or decibel reduction. A numeric LUFS master cannot prove speech clarity. [FFmpeg filter documentation](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#sidechaincompress), [Frame.io mixing workflow](https://blog.frame.io/2021/08/16/premiere-pro-audio-mixing-basics/).

**Delivery loudness, carefully stated:** Spotify documents **−14 LUFS integrated and below −1 dBTP** as *music mastering advice for Spotify*, with listener settings at −11/−14/−19 LUFS. It is **not evidence that YouTube, TikTok and every social feed require −14 LUFS**. Vid2's current **−14 LUFS / −1 dBTP** default is a reasonable explicit export preset for a short web video; label it as a project target and allow destination-specific presets. FFmpeg's `loudnorm` implements EBU R128 normalization, offers two-pass measurement, and accepts `I`, `LRA` and `TP`; measure the **encoded delivery** again because codec output can move peaks. `vid2 qa out.mp4 --timeline timeline.json --expect-audio --json` already checks integrated loudness within ±1 LU and delivered true peak against its target; a direct diagnostic is `ffmpeg -i out.mp4 -filter_complex ebur128=peak=true -f null -`. [Spotify loudness documentation](https://support.spotify.com/bj-en/artists/article/loudness-normalization/), [FFmpeg loudnorm and ebur128](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html), [vid2 QA](https://github.com/lidge-ai/vid2-gen/blob/main/structure/qa.md).

**Proposed sound QA:** export a cue sheet with onset/peak/end frames and stem type; flag duplicate impacts within the same semantic event, SFX that peak more than one frame away from a declared hard-sync point, voice overlapping a deliberate full-mix silence, and unmarked silence where audio was expected. Count cues per section, not merely per second; a sparse impact can be more forceful than a constant chain. This is a proposed decision rule. [Adobe cue-sheet guidance](https://www.adobe.com/uk/creativecloud/video/discover/sfx-for-video.html), [Berklee hard/soft sync distinction](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/5-film-scoring-concepts-to-know-for-your-first-movie-score/).

## 9. From moodboard to repeatable generated-shot style

Midjourney's current docs distinguish **Style Reference** (`--sref <image URL or code>`) from **Moodboards**, which are personalization profiles used with `--p <moodboard ID>` or a frozen `--p <code>`. Style weight is `--sw 0..1000` (default `100`) and **does not work with Moodboards**; Moodboard influence uses `--stylize` / `--s 0..1000` (default `100`). A moodboard's ID follows its latest revision; submitting an ID converts it to a versioned code. A seed is useful for controlled experiments, **not a reliable style lock across prompts**. These are version-sensitive flags; record the model version and the actual returned code with every generated asset. [Midjourney Style Reference](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32180011136653-Style-Reference), [Moodboards](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/39193335040013-Moodboards), [Seeds](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32604356340877-Seeds).

**Repeatable workflow:**

1. Make a compact board of approved palette, lighting, texture, lens/framing and negative examples. Write these as shot constraints; do not rely on the board name alone. A colorscript then assigns one palette/function to each scene. [Pixar colorscript](https://www.pixar.com/monsters-inc).
2. Pick **one fixed style route per sequence**: for example `--sref <approved-image-URL> --sw 100`, or `--p <frozen-moodboard-code> --s 100`. The literal `100` values are documented defaults, **not a guaranteed best setting**. Do not write `--moodboard` as a Midjourney prompt flag: the documented prompt syntax for a specific Moodboard is `--p`. [Style Reference](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32180011136653-Style-Reference), [Moodboards](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/39193335040013-Moodboards).
3. Hold model version, style code/reference, `--ar`, stylization, and core art-direction words stable while changing only the shot subject and composition. `--ar 16:9` or `--ar 9:16` matches common video canvases; Midjourney says to crop an image prompt to the target aspect ratio. A separate image prompt uses `--iw` (documented range **0–3** in V7 and V8.1), which controls **content-image** influence rather than `--sref` style influence. [Aspect ratio](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/31894244298125-Aspect-Ratio), [Image Prompts](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32040250122381-Image-Prompts).
4. Generate several candidates per shot, make a contact sheet, and reject outliers in palette, camera angle, materials, character/product identity and typography space. Store the approved image bytes, exact prompt, model/version, reference URL or code, profile code, seed (for provenance only), aspect ratio and output dimensions. Midjourney explicitly warns that references inspire rather than copy exactly and that seeds have weak consistency value. [Image Prompts](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32040250122381-Image-Prompts), [Seeds](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32604356340877-Seeds).
5. Grade the approved images as one sequence, inspect seams, and reserve **real capture** for product proof. Midjourney's style reference is an aesthetic control, not proof that the generated scene depicts the actual product. [Pixar grading workflow](https://renderman.pixar.com/the-grand-tour-compositing), [Google product-in-use guide](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/17033/youtube_ad_production_guide_external.pdf).

**Vid2 boundary:** the documented `generate` source currently goes through ima2-gen and hashes provider, prompt, options and seed-image bytes for cache identity. Midjourney flag examples above are **reference/prompt workflow guidance**, not a claim that vid2 or ima2 currently exposes Midjourney as a provider. Store provenance in a future style manifest or resolve Midjourney assets externally and import them as local media. [vid2 assets](https://github.com/lidge-ai/vid2-gen/blob/main/structure/assets.md).

## Implications for vid2-gen

Ranked by likely value to an agent that cannot inspect the finished film directly:

1. **Add a film-grammar report to `vid2 qa` or a read-only `vid2 analyze timeline.json` command.** Emit cut reasons, shot-duration distribution, text-reading hold, cue anchors and hard-sync frame deltas in JSON; these are the highest-value checks the agent can actually verify from a resolved timeline. [Salt/Cinemetrics](https://cinemetrics.uchicago.edu/article/9890e39c-ead0-49ff-a800-37d83723836a), [vid2 timing](https://github.com/lidge-ai/vid2-gen/blob/main/skills/vid2-timeline/references/time.md).
2. **Add typed beat/phrase/drop markers and a rhythm audit.** Surface song parts, bars and beats separately, compare cuts and impacts to declared intent, and accept an explicit `offbeat` annotation; this prevents accidental drift without enforcing metronomic cutting. [Apple Final Cut Pro](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/guide/final-cut-pro/ver65b55a2b7/mac), [Berklee](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/5-film-scoring-concepts-to-know-for-your-first-movie-score/).
3. **Add platform-specific safe-area masks and full-span text checks.** Evaluate moving backgrounds and reading hold after animation settles, especially for 9:16 exports; a generic 5% margin and one still cannot represent platform UI or changing contrast. [Google masks](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13547298?hl=en-AU), [TikTok masks](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/tiktok-auction-in-feed-ads?lang=en-GB), [Netflix timing](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/215758617-Timed-Text-Style-Guide-General-Requirements).
4. **Add an edit-intent sidecar or optional schema metadata.** Store `claim`, `proof`, `gazeTarget`, `matchAxis`, `actionEvent`, `syncIntent` and `reviewNeeded` per cut without altering render behavior; this makes Murch's priorities and match-cut claims reviewable. [Murch](https://www.filmsound.org/murch/interview-with-walter-murch.htm), [Netflix match-cut paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05766).
5. **Add a sound cue-sheet and stem-aware QA report.** Display onset/peak/end at exact frames, auto/authored collisions, voice ducking and delivered LUFS/true peak; the mixer exists, but its dramatic timing needs a readable receipt. [Adobe SFX guide](https://www.adobe.com/uk/creativecloud/video/discover/sfx-for-video.html), [FFmpeg filters](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html).
6. **Add a color-script and style-provenance manifest.** Compare scene thumbnails and seam color metrics, and pin prompt/reference/model/profile codes for generated shots; this converts moodboard taste into repeatable shot selection without claiming deterministic generation. [Pixar](https://www.pixar.com/monsters-inc), [Midjourney Moodboards](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/39193335040013-Moodboards).
7. **Add named motion presets with sourced token tables.** Offer Carbon/Material curves and durations as opt-in references, with frame-rounded values at the output fps, while retaining vid2's existing spring and easing controls; this gives agents a precise motion vocabulary without overwriting a film's style. [Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/), [Material 3](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Motion.md).
